## Validation & Kaggle

OOF AUC > Mean Fold AUC for estimating real-world performance.

Public LB is a noisy sample; Private LB is the true test score.

Optimize OOF, not Public LB.

OOF predictions are generated by models that never trained on the predicted rows.

A good CV setup produces OOF scores close to Private LB scores.

Large leaderboard gains without OOF gains are suspicious.


---

## Boosting


Boosting builds trees sequentially on residual errors.

Prediction = sum of predictions from all trees.

Many weak trees often outperform fewer strong trees.

Weak trees require more boosting rounds.

Strong trees require fewer boosting rounds but overfit more easily.

When best_iteration hits n_estimators repeatedly, the model may want more trees.


---

## Bias vs Variance


Simple models have high bias and low variance.

Complex models have low bias and high variance.

Overfitting is usually a variance problem.

Regularization trades variance for bias.

The best model sits at the bias-variance sweet spot.


---

## num_leaves


num_leaves controls how many regions a tree can create.

More leaves = more expressive trees.

More leaves != better generalization.

If increasing num_leaves hurts OOF, the model is fitting noise.

Small num_leaves encourages broad patterns.

Large num_leaves encourages specific patterns.


---

## min_child_samples


min_child_samples controls the minimum evidence required for a leaf.

Small leaves can memorize rare patterns.

Large leaves force the model to learn broad trends.

Increasing min_child_samples is a form of regularization.

If increasing min_child_samples improves OOF, the model was overfitting.

num_leaves controls region count; min_child_samples controls region size.


---

## n_estimators


n_estimators controls the number of boosting rounds.

Weak trees need more boosting rounds.

Strong trees need fewer boosting rounds.

Early stopping reveals how many trees the model actually wants.

Best_iteration approximates optimal ensemble size.


---

## Dataset Sampling


Dataset sampling permanently removes rows.

All trees train on the same sampled dataset.

Sampling reduces memory and preprocessing cost.

Rare signals may disappear after aggressive sampling.


---

## bagging_fraction


bagging_fraction samples rows separately for each tree.

Different trees see different subsets of data.

Bagging reduces tree correlation.

Bagging helps signal survive while noise averages out.

Bagging keeps most information available across the ensemble.

Bagging is often better than one-time sampling when data fits in memory.


---

## feature_fraction


feature_fraction samples columns separately for each tree.

Different trees see different subsets of features.

Feature subsampling reduces tree correlation.

Feature subsampling forces alternative predictive structures to be discovered.

A feature omitted from one tree can still appear in many others.

feature_fraction regularizes feature selection rather than tree structure.


---

## Tree Correlation


An ensemble benefits when trees make different mistakes.

Highly correlated trees add little ensemble value.

Reducing correlation is a major source of ensemble gains.

bagging_fraction and feature_fraction both reduce tree correlation.

Diverse trees usually generalize better than similar trees.


---

## Feature Importance


Gain importance measures how often and how effectively a feature wins split competitions.

Correlated features compete for the same gain.

Winning features receive credit; similar losing features receive little credit.

Low feature importance does not imply uselessness.

Feature importance can underestimate correlated predictors.


---

## Correlated Features


Correlated features often contain overlapping information.

Trees tend to repeatedly choose the slightly best correlated feature.

Feature subsampling allows alternative correlated features to contribute.

Multiple correlated features can represent the same signal differently.

Feature competition can hide useful features.


---

## Many Weak Signals vs Few Strong Signals


Few strong signals favor more complex trees.

Many weak signals favor stronger regularization.

Distributed feature importance often indicates many weak signals.

If regularization consistently improves OOF, noise is likely dominating complexity.

Broad patterns generalize better than highly specific patterns when signals are weak.


---

## Experimental Methodology


One variable should change per experiment.

Every experiment should test a hypothesis.

Score changes without a hypothesis teach little.

A failed hypothesis is still a successful experiment.

Look for consistent stories across multiple experiments.

Hyperparameter interactions are often more informative than individual scores.


---

## Santander-Specific Observations


Row statistics (mean/std/min/max) added little signal.

Hyperparameter tuning produced much larger gains than feature engineering.

Santander strongly prefers regularized trees.

num_leaves=16 significantly outperformed larger trees.

Large min_child_samples consistently improved OOF.

Aggressive feature subsampling improved OOF.

OOF closely tracked Private LB, indicating a reliable validation strategy.
